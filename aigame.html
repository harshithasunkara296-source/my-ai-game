📘 Algorithms Used in This Game

1. Minimax Algorithm

Minimax is a decision-making algorithm for two-player games. It explores possible future moves and assumes both players choose their best responses.

MAX: Tries to maximise the AI score. MIN: Tries to minimise the AI score. Here, O is MAX and X is MIN.

How it helps: It can find winning moves, block an opponent's winning move, and avoid losing when possible.

2. Alpha-Beta Pruning

Alpha-Beta Pruning improves Minimax by skipping branches that cannot change the final decision.

Alpha: Best score MAX can guarantee so far. Beta: Best score MIN can guarantee so far. When beta is less than or equal to alpha, remaining branches at that point can be skipped.

How it helps: It reduces unnecessary search work while preserving the optimal decision for the same search tree and evaluation rules.

3. Evaluation Scores

An AI win gets a positive score; an AI loss gets a negative score; a draw gets zero. Scores are adjusted by depth to favour faster wins and delay losses.

4. Difficulty

Easy: Random legal move. Medium: Selects among strong candidates. Hard: Selects the best candidate. Tic-Tac-Toe is small enough to search all remaining moves.

5. Live Calculations

The report shows each candidate cell, its score, the number of search nodes, pruning count, and selected move. The full log keeps these details for each AI turn until the game is reset.

6. AI vs AI

Both players use the selected algorithm and difficulty. You can observe their decisions, candidate scores, blocking moves, and final outcome.
